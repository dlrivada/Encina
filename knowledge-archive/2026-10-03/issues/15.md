## Decisions
- #15 proposed centralizing Event Sourcing orchestration into a new `Encina.EventSourcing` package (`AggregateOrchestrator`, `IAggregateRepository`, `IEventStore`) shared by `Encina.EventStoreDB` and `Encina.Marten`, mirroring the simple Orchestrator+Provider pattern already applied to Caching (#13) and Validation (#14).
- The maintainer closed it as superseded by a deeper analysis: EventStoreDB and Marten are **different strategies** (catch-up subscriptions/server-side JS projections vs. inline projections/async daemon/PostgreSQL-native features), not interchangeable providers under one Orchestrator. Closing comment: "these aren't interchangeable implementations — they're different architectural approaches" (source: dlrivada, issue close comment, verified via `gh issue view 15 --comments`).
- The closing comment named three replacement issues: Event Sourcing Strategy Pattern, Sagas Strategy Pattern, Messaging Strategy Pattern. These became spikes #17, #16, #18 respectively (all opened same day, all **CLOSED** — verified via `gh issue view {16,17,18}`).
- None of the three spikes produced an implemented Strategy→Orchestrator→Provider abstraction. Instead, the event-sourcing question was resolved differently and later: **ADR-027** (recorded 2026-09-22, decided in #17 (2025) and #321 (2026)) made `Encina.Marten` the sole event-sourcing provider and declared `Encina.EventStoreDB` deprecated. This is a narrower, simpler resolution than the Strategy pattern #17 proposed to investigate — the project ended up with one provider, so no orchestrator/strategy split was needed at all.

## Rejected alternatives
- Single Orchestrator + Provider abstraction across EventStoreDB and Marten (the original #15 proposal) — rejected because the two stores differ in fundamentals (stream versioning, projection lifecycle, tenancy) (source: #15 close comment; restated in ADR-027 "Alternatives rejected").
- Keep both EventStoreDB and Marten with a common Strategy abstraction — rejected in ADR-027 for the same reason, effectively also closing out spike #17's own "Option A" without a written PoC.

## Rules and lessons
- When evaluating patterns for subsystems with heterogeneous backend capabilities, first ask whether the backends are interchangeable providers (Caching, Validation) or fundamentally different strategies (Event Sourcing, and by the same reasoning, Sagas and Messaging Transports per #16/#18) — this became the general principle cited by ADR-027.
- A spike opened to "design the strategy selection API" (#17) does not have to end in that API: it can end in eliminating one side of the choice instead (dropping EventStoreDB), which is a valid and simpler outcome, but the spike's own issue and comments carry none of that reasoning — the decision is recorded only in ADR-027, written down nine months later "from the project history (Historian pass)".
