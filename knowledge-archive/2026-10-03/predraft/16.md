## Decisions
- **Reject Strategy Pattern**: It was decided NOT to implement the Strategy pattern for Sagas. Instead, the current architecture keeping Orchestration and Choreography as separate, distinct systems is maintained. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **Rationale for Rejection**: Orchestration and Choreography are fundamentally different; unifying them under a common interface would be artificial. Abstraction hides important architectural decisions that users must understand. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **Strategy Selection Scope**: Strategy selection is determined to be **application-wide**. Users must choose one approach for their entire application to avoid cognitive overhead and DI complexity. Mixing both in one application is discouraged, with separate bounded contexts as the only exception. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **Migration Policy**: There is **no automatic migration** path between strategies. They are considered fundamentally incompatible (Orchestration uses `CurrentStep`/`Data`, Choreography uses `Events[]`/`Compensations[]`). Switching requires completing/compensating pending sagas, rewriting definitions, and changing configuration. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **Deliverable Fulfillment**: The spike's deliverable of an ADR/documentation was fulfilled by creating `docs/messaging/sagas.md`, `docs/messaging/index.md`, and updating TOC files. Source: comment by dlrivada.

## Rejected alternatives
- **Option A: Strategy + Orchestrator Pattern**: Rejected because it implies unifying fundamentally different architectural patterns under a single interface, which was deemed artificial. Source: Issue body (Options to Evaluate) and comment by dlrivada.
- **Option B: Ship One Strategy, Add Second Later**: Implicitly superseded by the decision to maintain the *existing* two separate implementations rather than consolidating into one and adding the other. The spike concluded both already exist and are distinct. Source: Issue body (Options to Evaluate) and comment by dlrivada.
- **Option C: Keep Current Single Implementation**: Modified/Refined. The decision was not just to keep the code as-is, but to explicitly *document* the separation and enforce the "application-wide" choice constraint, rejecting the idea that they are interchangeable or that a unified API is desirable. Source: comment by dlrivada.

## Rules and lessons
- **Architectural Orthogonality**: Do not unify disparate architectural patterns (like Orchestration vs Choreography) into a single Strategy interface if their internal state models and compensation mechanisms are incompatible. Explicit choice over implicit abstraction. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **No Cross-Strategy Migration**: If two architectural patterns involve different state models (`CurrentStep` vs `Events[]`), do not attempt automatic migration. Treat them as distinct lifecycles. Source: comment by dlrivada.
- **Application-Wide Consistency**: When offering mutually exclusive architectural patterns, enforce a single choice at the application level to prevent complexity and confusion. Source: comment by dlrivada.

## Candidate destinations
- **docs**: The primary outcome is the creation of `docs/messaging/sagas.md` and related index/TOC files. This content belongs permanently in the documentation set.
- **adr**: While the issue was a spike, the decision "Keep Orchestration and Choreography Separate" with specific rationale (incompatibility, artificial unification) is a candidate for a formal ADR to record the architectural constraint.
- **reviewer-checklist**: Reviewers should check that new Saga implementations do not attempt to bridge Orchestration and Choreography state models, and that DI registrations respect the application-wide selection.
