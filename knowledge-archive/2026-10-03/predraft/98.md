## Decisions
- Branch protection rules for `main` were already in place with required PR reviews (1 approval), code-owner review, linear history, no force-push, conversation resolution, and required checks. (Source: comment by dlrivada)
- Two corrections were applied under SPEC-000 DEC-006: the required-check list was realigned with actual `ci.yml` jobs by introducing a new always-run 'ci-result' summary job to replace references to a non-existent 'Build' check and a retired SQLite EF shard. (Source: comment by dlrivada)
- The `enforce_admins` setting was enabled to ensure branch protection applies to all users, including maintainers. (Source: comment by dlrivada)
- Agents must deliver changes only through pull requests, as enforced by SPEC-000 INV-006. (Source: comment by dlrivada)

## Rejected alternatives
- No branch protection: Rejected as risky due to lack of review process and ease of introducing bugs. (Source: issue body)
- Require multiple reviewers (2+): Rejected as too restrictive for a single-maintainer project in its initial phase. (Source: issue body)
- Require all CI checks: Rejected as too rigid, acknowledging that some checks may be optional. (Source: issue body)

## Rules and lessons
- Required status checks in branch protection must strictly correspond to existing and active CI workflow jobs to avoid blocking merges with phantom or retired checks. (Source: comment by dlrivada)
- A summary job (e.g., 'ci-result') can be used to aggregate CI status for branch protection requirements when individual job names are volatile or complex. (Source: comment by dlrivada)
- Branch protection rules should enforce `enforce_admins` to prevent maintainers from bypassing review and CI processes. (Source: comment by dlrivada)

## Candidate destinations
