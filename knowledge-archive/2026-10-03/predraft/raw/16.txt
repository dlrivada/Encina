### Why Strategies, Not Just Providers?

| Aspect | Choreography | Orchestration |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **Coordination** | Decentralized (events) | Centralized (saga coordinator) |
| **Coupling** | Loose (services don't know each other) | Tighter (orchestrator knows all steps) |
| **Failure handling** | Event-driven compensation | Explicit rollback commands |
| **Visibility** | Distributed (harder to trace) | Centralized (easy to monitor) |
| **Complexity** | Simpler services, complex overall | Complex orchestrator, simpler services |

These are **architectural decisions**, not implementation details.

## Decision: Keep Orchestration and Choreography Separate

After analysis, we've decided **NOT to implement the Strategy pattern** for Sagas. Instead, we'll keep the current architecture with improved documentation.

### Why Not Strategy Pattern?

1. **Orchestration and Choreography are fundamentally different** - unifying them under a common interface would be artificial
2. **Users must understand the differences** before choosing - abstracting them hides important architectural decisions
3. **Current implementation already works** - two separate, well-defined systems

### Answers to Open Questions

**Q: Should strategy selection be per-module or application-wide?**
> **Answer: Application-wide** - Users choose ONE approach for their application. Mixing both creates cognitive overhead and DI complexity. The rare case of needing both can use separate bounded contexts.

**Q: How do we handle migration if a user wants to switch strategies?**
> **Answer: No automatic migration** - These are fundamentally incompatible:
> - Orchestration: `CurrentStep`, `Data`, sequential compensation
> - Choreography: `Events[]`, `Compensations[]`, event-driven compensation
>
> Migration requires:
> 1. Complete or compensate all pending sagas
> 2. Rewrite saga definitions for new strategy
> 3. Change configuration
