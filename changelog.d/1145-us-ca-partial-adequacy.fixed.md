- `RegionRegistry.US` and `RegionRegistry.CA` no longer count as adequate for every recipient: the EU-US Data Privacy Framework only covers DPF-certified US organisations, and Canada's adequacy finding only covers PIPEDA-covered commercial organisations. `Region.RequiresRecipientCertification` flags both regions; `IAdequacyDecisionProvider.HasAdequacy`, `ICrossBorderTransferValidator.ValidateTransferAsync`, and `TransferRequest.IsRecipientCertified` (Encina.Compliance.CrossBorderTransfer) take an explicit recipient-certification confirmation, defaulting to "not adequate" without it (#1145).
- New `IRecipientCertificationResolver` (Encina.Compliance.DataResidency) lets an application confirm, per request, whether a US or Canadian recipient is certified/in-scope under the DPF or PIPEDA. `AddEncinaDataResidency` and `AddEncinaCrossBorderTransfer` register the default `NullRecipientCertificationResolver`, which always answers `false` (fail closed), via `TryAddSingleton` so applications can override it (#1145).
- `TransferBlockingPipelineBehavior` resolves the destination region's certification through `IRecipientCertificationResolver` before validating the transfer, instead of always treating the recipient as uncertified. `DataResidencyPipelineBehavior` does the same for its adequacy check, and now distinguishes "no adequacy decision" from "adequacy decision needs recipient certification" in its denial message (#1145).
- New `TransferBasis.DataPrivacyFramework` (Encina.Compliance.CrossBorderTransfer) reports a US transfer allowed through the EU-US Data Privacy Framework separately from the generic `TransferBasis.AdequacyDecision`; it applies only to certified US recipients, not to Canada or any other partial-adequacy region (#1145).
